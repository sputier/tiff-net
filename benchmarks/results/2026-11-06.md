# Measurements of 2026-11-06

Reference machine: the 8-core laptop of the review (x64, AVX2, .NET 10.0, Windows 11), warm cache, best of 5 to 15 runs, other
work running (variations of about 10 %). Images of 24 MP (6 000 x 4 000), smooth with noise as astronomical frames are: u16
grey, f32 RGB. Reproduced by `TargetBenchmarks` (BenchmarkDotNet) and, for JPEG, by `tools/bench_jpeg.py` and `JpegBenchmarks`.

## JPEG against libjpeg-turbo (12 MP, quality 90, one thread)

| Image | libjpeg-turbo (Pillow) | Eleris.TiffNet | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| grey | 34 ms | 38 ms | 1.1 |
| YCbCr 4:4:4 | 82 ms | 98 ms | 1.2 |
| YCbCr 4:2:0 | 48 ms | 65 ms | 1.35 |
| progressive 4:2:0 | 145 ms | 190 ms | 1.3 |

What brought them there: an AVX2 islow IDCT (bit-exact with the scalar one; the scalar one stays where AVX2 is missing, ARM
included), an 11-bit Huffman lookup with whole coefficients, an 8-byte bit refill, colour converted row by row.

## After the writer's review (bands aligned on the source, the calling thread encoding)

`TargetBenchmarks`, short job, the machine otherwise idle:

| Scenario | 1 thread | 8 threads | Allocated |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Write u16 24 MP, Deflate + predictor | 0.60 s | 0.11 s | 64 MB |
| Write f32 RGB 24 MP, Deflate + predictor | 4.4 s | 0.79 s | 448 MB (600 MB before) |
| Read u16 24 MP, Deflate + predictor | 157 ms (306 MB/s) | 31 ms (1.5 GB/s) | 49 MB |
| Read f32 RGB 24 MP, Deflate + floating point predictor | 640 ms (450 MB/s) | 129 ms (2.2 GB/s) | 279 MB |
| Read u16 24 MP, none | 12 ms (4.0 GB/s) | 13 ms | 46 MB |

The write of f32 RGB meets its target at the default level (`Optimal`) with some margin on an idle machine; under load it
comes close to 1 s; `Fastest` is the level for a write that must be quicker.

## The level `Fastest`

`TargetBenchmarks` (scenario `f32-rgb-fastest`), short job; file sizes from the same images written to a stream:

| Image | Level | 1 thread | 8 threads | File |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| f32 RGB 24 MP | `Optimal` | 4.40 s | 0.81 s | 193.5 MB |
| f32 RGB 24 MP | `Fastest` | 1.74 s | 0.35 s | 221.8 MB (+15 %) |
| u16 24 MP | `Optimal` | 0.58 s | 0.11 s | 23.8 MB |
| u16 24 MP | `Fastest` | 0.26 s | 0.06 s | 36.3 MB (+52 %) |

`Fastest` writes 2.3 to 2.5 times faster, files 15 to 50 % larger. Deflate at `Optimal` compresses the noisy f32 data at about
60 MB/s per core (288 MB in 4.4 s).

## The paths of real files (issues #78 to #82)

`PathBenchmarks`, 24 MP uncompressed, every core: the cost of unpacking and placing samples, not of a codec. Before: the
code of 0.1.0; after: rows converted in bulk, results not cleared, low depths read through a window of 64 bits.

| Source | Read as | Before | After |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| u16 RGB, interleaved | raw | 12 ms | 4.8 ms |
| u16 RGB, interleaved | planar (a FITS cube) | 26 ms | 11 ms |
| u16 RGB, interleaved | float, normalised | 38 ms | 9.6 ms |
| u16 RGB, interleaved | double | 53 ms | 17 ms |
| u16 RGB, planar | planar | 28 ms | 5.6 ms |
| u16 RGB, planar | raw (interleaved) | 29 ms | 14 ms |
| u16 RGB, planar | double (interleaved) | 82 ms | 54 ms |
| u16 grey, big endian | raw | 4.9 ms | 2.1 ms |
| u16 grey, big endian | float, normalised | 15 ms | 4.0 ms |
| 12-bit grey | raw | 44 ms | 6.1 ms |
| 12-bit grey | double | 63 ms | 11 ms |

A planar file read in interleaved order is still the slowest: each plane is placed apart, so the destination is written once
per channel. Reading it as planar (the order it is stored in) is the fast path.

`PredictorBenchmarks`, undoing the predictors on 4 000 rows of 6 000 pixels, one thread:

| Rows | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| f32 RGB, floating point predictor | 218 ms | 32 ms |
| f32 grey, floating point predictor | 63 ms | 13 ms |
| u16 RGB, horizontal | 38 ms | 9 ms |
| u16 grey, horizontal | 11 ms | 3.8 ms |

`TargetBenchmarks`: the read of f32 RGB Deflate takes 446 ms on one thread (640 ms before) and 109 ms on every core (129 ms,
2.6 GB/s); the other reads and the writes, bound by Deflate, are unchanged.

## Deflate levels and strategies (issue #83)

Predicted strips of 256 KB of a 24 MP frame, one thread, `System.IO.Compression` (zlib-ng); ratio = compressed / raw.

| Data | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 | Level 4 | Level 6 (`Optimal`) | Level 9 (`SmallestSize`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| f32 RGB, noisy | 173 MB/s, 0.767 | 114 MB/s, 0.679 | 81 MB/s, 0.679 | 80 MB/s, 0.678 | 45 MB/s, 0.676 | 30 MB/s, 0.681 |
| u16, noisy | 215 MB/s, 0.753 | 122 MB/s, 0.499 | 113 MB/s, 0.493 | 98 MB/s, 0.493 | 70 MB/s, 0.493 | 35 MB/s, 0.508 |
| f32 RGB, little noise | 232 MB/s, 0.642 | 156 MB/s, 0.588 | 111 MB/s, 0.578 | 103 MB/s, 0.573 | 76 MB/s, 0.566 | 39 MB/s, 0.563 |
| u16, little noise | 372 MB/s, 0.464 | 242 MB/s, 0.264 | 176 MB/s, 0.230 | 115 MB/s, 0.213 | 48 MB/s, 0.199 | 11 MB/s, 0.205 |

- `CompressionStrategy.Filtered`, which zlib recommends for filtered data, was slower at every level for the same sizes (the
  same table with it: 124/87/66/61/50/35 MB/s for f32 RGB, noisy): the default strategy is kept.
- Level 6 stays `Optimal`: level 2 is 1.7 to 5 times faster with the same size on noisy data, but a third larger on smooth
  16-bit data. Level 9 is rarely smaller than 6 with zlib-ng, and slower: documented on `SmallestSize`.

LZW (`LzwBenchmarks`, 8 MB of 16-bit samples, medium job): encoding with one packed table and codes emitted without a closure
takes 58 ms for noisy data (65 ms before) and 24 ms for flat data (27 ms); decoding refills 8 bytes at once, within the noise
of the measurement for noisy data (33 ms), where copying the strings dominates.

## Files of many small strips (issue #85)

`PathBenchmarks`, 24 MP u16 grey in 4 000 strips of one row (12 KB, as libtiff writes strips of about 8 KB), read from a file
in the cache of the system, every core:

| Strips | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| uncompressed | 32.6 ms | 9.6 ms (strips that follow each other read in one call, up to 1 MB) |
| Deflate | 65 ms | 65 ms |

A Deflate strip of one row costs about 16 µs (its zlib stream and its read): the same image in strips of 256 KB reads in about
half the time. Reading the compressed bytes of several small strips at once would be the next step.

## JPEG on processors without AVX2 (issue #86)

`JpegBenchmarks` (decoding the components, 12 MP, one thread) with `DOTNET_EnableAVX2=0`, which stands for ARM (Android, Apple
Silicon) and older x64: the islow IDCT on 128-bit vectors (SSE2 or AdvSimd) instead of the scalar one.

| Image | Scalar IDCT | 128-bit IDCT | AVX2 IDCT (for reference) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| grey | 56 ms | 42 ms | 38 ms |
| YCbCr 4:4:4 | 151 ms | 100 ms | 87 ms |
| YCbCr 4:2:0 | 71 ms | 52 ms | 57 ms |
| progressive 4:2:0 | 199 ms | 179 ms | 177 ms |

Upsampling and the colour conversion take 15 to 20 % of the decoding of a colour image (`ToImage` against `Components`):
vectorising them, and reusing the planes of the components between blocks, is left for later.
